## Labels: floor versus defensible numerical claims

There is no universal sample size that proves compliance or model quality. Choose the denominator and claim first. Precision/recall concern labeled control firings, not the legal validity of an entire communication. Separate block, review and transform false interventions. The recommendation pack always routes to review; an aggregate review rate is therefore workflow load, not evidence of detector precision.

Unchanged T-833 requirements: >=20 positive and >=20 hard-negative holdout cases per named scored semantic family; >=2 independently worded cases for every reachable action; >=2 clear/defect counterfactual pairs for each intervening control; known-present/known-absent/unknown coverage for each permitted material applicability branch; adversarial, invalid schema, wrong universe/jurisdiction, conflicting controls and DLP compatibility. These requirements overlap but cannot be replaced by an overall 40-case pack count. Missing/invalid/duplicate predictions stay failures in frozen eligible denominators. Two reviewers independently label actions, control results and exact citations, with named adjudication and custodian evidence.

For A, propose an additional pilot evidence floor of **60 positive rule-defect cases plus 60 clear/hard-negative cases per commercially claimed pack**, with at least the existing deterministic/action/control/applicability gates. This is a proposed profile rule, not an already frozen requirement. Every control being advertised still needs its own evidence coverage. Dataset size increases if the coverage matrix cannot fit those 120 cases. Pilot cases remain independent synthetic cases; domain applicability/counsel approval is separate.

For B's three currently named families, the semantic floor alone is 120 adjudicated cases, with 240 reviewer label decisions plus adjudication; deterministic and other coverage must be added. Extra named semantic families require another 20+20 each. Publishing 20/20 alone allows only a small-dataset result, not “>=99% reliable.”

Computed 95% Wilson bounds for an all-success or zero-error slice:

| Eligible n | All-success lower bound | Zero-error upper bound |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | 83.89% | 16.11% |
| 60 | 93.98% | 6.02% |
| 100 | 96.30% | 3.70% |
| 300 | 98.74% | 1.26% |

These intervals describe the assumed sampling model; synthetic/template-correlated cases do not establish a real-traffic distribution. For a separate one-sided exact binomial calculation, 0/300 errors gives an upper 95% bound about 0.99%; do not mix that with the contract's two-sided Wilson report or promote it to a field-reliability claim. Always publish numerator/denominator, task family, dataset digest and telemetry coverage. Precision needs enough predicted positives; do not invent precision when its denominator is zero. Review rate and p50/p95 require the actual pinned execution path, measured repetitions and failures; no GPU/CPU mixing or fabricated cost.

## B: compressed schedule and stop condition

This is a capacity estimate, not an observed throughput or ETA. Staff in parallel by pack; do not assume filling reviewer ID fields creates independence. If actors/coverage are not confirmed by September 13 evening, choose A. Freeze by September 14 evening, run/review by September 15, deploy only accepted scope September 16.
